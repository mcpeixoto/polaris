# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people want to work with. Assume the person on the other end is competent and
acting in good faith, and say what you mean without being unpleasant about it.

## What this means in practice

**Critique the work, not the person.** "This will deadlock when two clients retry at once"
is useful. "Did you even think about concurrency?" is the same information delivered so
that the recipient has to get past it before they can act on it.

**Being right is not a licence.** Technical correctness does not excuse contempt, and a
correct comment delivered badly usually gets argued with rather than acted on — which
means it fails at the only thing it was for.

**Assume competence.** People come to this project from different backgrounds and with
different amounts of context. Somebody who does not know how our sync engine mints versions
has not failed a test; they have not read that file yet.

**Disagree, then commit.** Argue the design as hard as you like before the decision. Once
it is made and written down, relitigating it in every subsequent PR is a way of not
accepting it.

**Let people change their mind.** Somebody who reverses a position under a good argument
has done the right thing. Treating that as a win to be pointed at makes the next person
dig in instead.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, demeaning comments about who somebody is rather than what
they wrote, unwelcome sexual attention, deliberate intimidation, publishing private
information, and sustained disruption. This applies in the repository, in issues, in pull
requests, in chat, and at any event where you are representing the project.

## Reporting

Email **conduct@peixotolabs.com**. Reports go to the maintainers and are kept confidential.
You will get an acknowledgement within 72 hours.

If your report concerns a maintainer, say so — it will be handled by someone else.

## What happens next

Depending on severity: a private word, a public correction, a temporary ban, or a permanent
one. We will tell you what we decided and why. We will not pretend a first offence and a
pattern are the same thing, and we will not pretend that a valuable contributor gets more
latitude than anybody else — that trade is exactly how a project becomes unpleasant to be
part of.

## Scope

This applies to everyone: contributors, maintainers, and users interacting with the
project's spaces.
